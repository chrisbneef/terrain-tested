## What is a wet clutch?

A wet clutch runs bathed in oil. In most motorcycles, dirt bikes and many ATVs, the engine, the transmission and the clutch all share one oil supply, called a shared sump. A car keeps these separate: engine oil in the engine, a different fluid in the transmission, and a dry clutch or a torque converter.

That one design choice changes what the oil has to do.

## Why can car oil cause clutch slip?

A wet clutch works by friction between its plates. The oil between those plates has to allow them to grip firmly when the clutch is engaged.

Many modern passenger car oils are formulated to reduce friction inside the engine to improve fuel economy. The friction modifiers that do this can also reduce the grip between clutch plates. The result can be a clutch that slips under power, engages inconsistently, or glazes over time.

## What does JASO MA or MA2 mean?

JASO is the Japanese Automotive Standards Organization. Its T 903 standard tests four-stroke motorcycle oils for clutch friction performance and sorts them into categories:

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| JASO MA | Suitable for wet clutches |
| JASO MA1 | Wet clutch suitable, lower end of the MA friction range |
| JASO MA2 | Wet clutch suitable, higher end of the MA friction range |
| JASO MB | Lower friction, for engines without a wet clutch in the oil, such as many scooters |

If your manual calls for JASO MA or MA2, look for that marking on the bottle. An oil marked only JASO MB is not meant for a wet clutch.

## Is the clutch the only reason?

No. In a shared sump the transmission gears run in the engine oil, and gears are very good at shearing oil. Shear breaks down the additives that hold a multigrade oil at its hot viscosity, so the oil thins out. Motorcycle oils are formulated to resist that shear and stay in grade.

Bikes also tend to rev higher and run hotter per unit of oil than cars do, and many are air-cooled. All of that asks more of the oil.

## What about machines with separate transmissions?

Some motorcycles, many UTVs and most two-stroke dirt bikes keep the transmission or clutch oil separate from the engine. In those machines each compartment has its own specification. The manual will list them separately, and the rule is the same: match the specification for each one.

## How do you choose the right oil?

1. Look up the viscosity grade and the JASO or API specification in your service manual.
2. Confirm whether the clutch shares oil with the engine.
3. Choose an oil that states it meets that specification on the label.
