## Why is price per quart the wrong comparison?

Because you do not pay for oil once. You pay for every oil change the machine will ever get, and each change costs more than the oil: there is a filter, someone's time, disposal, and the hours the machine is not working.

An oil that costs more per quart but safely runs longer between changes can cost less over a season. An oil that is cheap per quart but has to be changed twice as often may not be cheap at all.

## How do you work out cost per hour?

Add up everything one oil change costs, then divide by the hours you run between changes.

**Cost per hour = (oil + filter + labor + downtime) ÷ hours between changes**

| Item | What to count |
| --- | --- |
| Oil | The full fill, at what you actually pay |
| Filter | Oil filter, plus any others changed at the same service |
| Labor | Time to do the service, at your shop rate or your own hourly value |
| Downtime | What the machine would have earned in the hours it sat |
| Hours between changes | The interval you really run, not the one you plan to |

Run the numbers once for the oil you use now and once for the oil you are considering. For vehicles tracked in miles, use miles in place of hours.

## When does a synthetic pay for itself?

In my own experience, the higher upfront cost mostly disappears once longer service intervals are counted. It tends to pay off fastest when:

- **Downtime is expensive.** For a working machine, the parked hours often cost more than the oil.
- **The machine works hard.** High heat, heavy load and long hours are where synthetic base oils hold up better.
- **Service is a hassle.** Fewer changes matter more when the machine is remote or the service takes a long time.
- **A failure would be costly.** Protection for an expensive engine is cheap insurance.

## When is it not worth it?

A premium oil is not automatically the right call. If a machine runs only a few hours a year and gets its oil changed annually regardless, a longer drain capability gives you nothing. If the engine leaks or burns oil, fix that first. And if dirt or fuel is getting into the oil, contamination will end the interval early no matter how good the oil is.

## How far can you extend an interval?

Start from the equipment manufacturer's recommendation and the oil maker's recommendation for that product. While a machine is under warranty, know what the warranty requires for maintenance and keep your records.

The reliable way to go beyond a standard interval is [oil analysis](/field-notes/what-is-oil-analysis/). A lab report on your used oil shows whether it still has life left and whether the engine is wearing normally. That turns an extended interval from a guess into a decision backed by evidence from your own machine.
